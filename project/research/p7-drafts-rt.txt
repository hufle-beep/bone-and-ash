supper_roof:2:4 | Mei Lin bets you that the lamp of the widow by the well will go out before the dyer's. ¶ "Stakes?" ¶ "The loser carries the water in the morning. Both buckets." She settles on her elbows to watch the lane. "The widow sleeps early. She likes to be up before anyone else's faults are." ¶ "And the dyer?" ¶ "Owes the miller. He sits up doing sums until they come out differently." ¶ You back the dyer's lamp to go first, on principle. Both burn on. A shutter creaks, a basin is emptied into the lane, and the widow's window goes dark. ¶ "You've watched this lane before." ¶ "Every night since I could climb." She says it kindly, which is worse. "It was never a bet. It was a lesson."
supper_roof:2:4:r | The dyer's lamp outlasts you both, and his sums come out no better for the extra oil. On the ladder she tells you where the yoke hangs and which bucket leaks, as a kindness to the defeated. You go in like a man with water to carry.
supper_roof:2:5 | Mei Lin examines you on the contents of the herb loft, in the dark, by smell alone. ¶ She passes the sprigs across one at a time. You get the mugwort. You get the dried orange peel, which she says a child would. The third is bitter and faintly of cellars, and you name the mould growing on it instead, with its habits. ¶ "That is a fault in the stock," she says. "You are not supposed to praise it." ¶ "It's doing very well." ¶ "It will be out by morning." She takes the sprig back and sniffs it herself, frowning. "Which shelf?" ¶ "Third. By the window. It likes the damp." ¶ "So do you, it seems. You sleep there."
supper_roof:2:5:r | She runs out of sprigs before you run out of wrong answers. You have failed four, every one a plain leaf, and she goes down the ladder naming them at you like a debt. You follow her in, owing condolences to the third shelf.
supper_roof:2:6:r | The chestnuts run out before the rules do. There is a fourth, about the ladder after dark, and you break it together, rung by rung. At the bottom she folds the blanket with great correctness, as evidence. You go in smelling of chestnut smoke, which is also evidence.
