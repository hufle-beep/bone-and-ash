### TR-N
Across the fire Mei Lin has not moved. She has watched tribulations burn people down to their sandals, and she is still counting on her fingers for strokes that are not coming. "It left," she says. "They don't leave."

Ya prods the scorched moss with the butt of her spear, sniffs it, and grins at you with all her teeth. "The sky ran from you, scholar. I have chased things for less."

"It had somewhere to be," you say.

### TR-D
Mei Lin is standing where she stood when the clouds came, a hand still raised to shield her eyes from a flash that never arrived. "I have seen that burn people," she says. "I have never seen it change its mind." Then she laughs, once, sounding surprised at herself.

Ya crouches over the scorched moss, pokes it, and looks you up and down, slowly. "Heaven sniffed you and walked off. What do you smell of, scholar?"

"Mostly cat," you say.

### TR-H
Two heads come up through the crack below you, one after the other. Mei Lin looks at the sky, then at the scorch in the dead bark, then at you, unburnt and sitting. "I came up to collect what was left," she says. "I brought a cloth." She holds it up, as evidence.

Ya hauls herself onto the lip, puts a bare foot on the scorch and finds it cold. "Do it again," she says. "I was climbing. I missed the good part."

"That was the good part," you say.

### L7
Morning. Axes, hammers, and the work-chant, which since breakfast keeps the beat of the dwarves' song. The hunters hum the low part, gravely and wrong, and lift their stakes to it.

### LL
Old Wen reads it on his feet in the elder's house, the council in a ring around him, holding the scroll at arm's length as he would hold a debt.

*The Crimson Lotus sets down the report of inner disciple Lu Han. At the tribute of Ashfall a stranger raised his hand against the Sect's collector and was beaten. The Sect sets down what that village calls him: Bone-Maker. It sets down that the bone he works was never carved and came out of no beast. It sets down that what the disciple's hand found in him, when it struck, does not agree with what the disciple's eyes had seen, and that he cannot account for it. The year granted is withdrawn. Outer Elder Shen Qiu comes to Ashfall on the twenty-first day, to collect the Bone-Maker with his own hands.*

Old Wen rolls the scroll shut and keeps hold of it, there being nowhere in the room fit to put it down. Nobody in the ring looks at you. They look at the floor in front of you, with care.

"The Sect does not send an Elder to carry what a disciple could drag," says Old Hu, the head hunter, to the floor.

"I lost that fight," you say. "I was told losing would be quieter."

Nobody tells you otherwise. Under your shirt, Lu Han's boot print has begun to itch.
