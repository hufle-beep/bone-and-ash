tb:mei30 | Under the table, Mei Lin's foot comes to rest on top of yours and stays there. Above the table she is a dutiful daughter, asking her mother whether the well rope will see out the month. You keep your face in order, which is the one skill your education did give you, and leave your foot where it is. Her mother says the rope will do. Mei Lin presses down once, lightly, as one presses a seal onto a letter, and goes on being a credit to the house.
tb:mei72 | Mei Lin is humming, and the tune is one of yours. It gets out of your forearm when you are not minding it, quietly, and she has it nearly right, down to the place where the drummer loses patience. She does not seem to know she is doing it. Her mother knows. She hears out a whole verse with her hands folded, looks at her daughter, and then at you, for about as long as it takes to price a goat. "That is not a song of this lane," she says. "See that you learn how it ends." Mei Lin stops, colours, and starts over, louder.
tb:ya45:camp | Ya picks up her sleeping hide, spear and all, puts it down hard against your bedroll, and sits on it to look at you. "I have thought about it, scholar," she says, loud enough for the whole camp. "I want you. I am telling you so that you are not surprised." You thank her for the notice. Over the flames Mei Lin asks whether Ya would care to put that in writing. Ya, who cannot write, says writing is for people who forget what they want, and goes on looking.
tb:ya72:camp | Ya wets her thumb, rubs it in the cold char at the fire's edge, and draws a line under your left eye, where her own scar runs. She is not flirting; she does it as she would check a spearhead, frowning, and leans away to judge the work. "There. Now what is out there knows whose you are." You ask what that makes you. "Mine," she says. "Not to eat. To keep. It is different, scholar. Ask the herb girl, she has the words." Mei Lin tells you it is crooked. Nobody offers to wipe it off, and you do not ask.
tb:ya45:night | Ya, over on the shrine's ridge, sees you looking and does not duck. She stands up instead, tall on the skyline, points the spear at your window, then lays her free hand flat on her own chest and waits to be understood. It is not a subtle language. You raise one hand, which commits you to nothing, and she sits down satisfied, as a hunter does who has marked where the deer beds.
tb:ya72:night | While you look, the figure over the lane turns its back on your window and settles facing the road, spear laid over its knees. It takes you a little while to read it. Ya was watching you; now she is watching for you, as she would sit over her own fire and her own kin. You have, without any paperwork, been moved from quarry to household. The loft feels smaller, and better guarded.
