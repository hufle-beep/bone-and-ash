fo_supper:9:4 | There is a basin of water on the step when Ya arrives, with a folded cloth beside it, and Mei Lin's mother standing over both as still as a magistrate. Ya looks at her own feet, which have brought a fair portion of the forest floor with them. She looks at the basin. She picks it up, drinks half, and hands it back. ¶ The mother receives it in both hands. She says nothing a guest could be shamed by; she lays the cloth by Ya's place, squared, and serves the millet. Mei Lin has found a reason to look into the pickle jar and is staying there. ¶ Ya eats two bowls and considers the cook over the rim of a third. "A woman who cooks like this, somebody steals her," she says. "Why has nobody stolen you?" Then, to you: "Scholar. Tell her it's praise." She wipes her fingers on the cloth, pleased to have found its use.
fo_supper:9:4:away | Supper. Somewhere a dog barks. Everyone at the table puts their bowl down.
fo_supper:9:5 | The spear has been left at the door; that much Mei Lin's mother has won, by standing in front of it. The necklace she has not won. It comes to the table on Ya and rattles against the bowl's rim each time she leans in, forty-odd teeth of several species keeping time with the meal. ¶ The mother, who holds that a guest is owed conversation with her rice, asks in her gravest company voice what beasts they came from. Mei Lin shuts her eyes. ¶ Ya is glad to be asked. She goes along the string with her thumb. "Boar. Boar. Marsh cat. That one is mine, it came out in a fall. This one was a man's. He started it." ¶ She looks across at you, and judges. "Yours are good, scholar. Flat. I'd leave yours in." Then she hooks a finger in her cheek and leans over the pickles, so that the mother may inspect the gap.
fo_supper:9:5:away | Supper. The mother trims the lamp low and listens between mouthfuls.
fo_supper:9:6 | Ya stops inside the door, puts her heels together, and tells the roof beam, in the voice of a child reciting: "This unworthy guest thanks the house for its fire and its rice." Every word is in its place. Mei Lin, at the stove, watches her as a gambler watches the last die. ¶ Her mother has put down the bowls. She returns, to its full depth, the bow that was not made, and says that the house is honoured; and it is plain she means it, which is worse for everybody concerned. ¶ Ya sits, well satisfied, and eats. Halfway down the bowl a thought overtakes her. "Unworthy," she says. "She had me say unworthy. Scholar. Is that their word for bad?" Yuki, who knows precisely what it is the word for, has turned to hear you answer. ¶ Mei Lin has begun to laugh, which will not help her. Ya is already half off her stool.
fo_supper:9:6:away | Supper. Mei Lin talks about the weather, and her mother lets her.
# notes
- 9:4 assumes Ya's feet carry "forest floor" (I kept the Blackroot's name out, since the plan for it must not be leaned on) and that the mother's house has a step at the door.
- 9:5 assumes Ya has lost a tooth of her own and wears it, that one tooth on the string is a man's, and that "marsh cat" is a beast this world has; swap the beast if not. The spear left at the door does not contradict the old third text if read in any order, but it is a small win for the mother that canon may not want.
- 9:6 assumes Mei Lin coached Ya in the guest's thanks off screen (her tease, her trouble). The wording of the formal thanks is mine; replace with the house's form if lore.md has one. Ya ends half off her stool: a threat, no fight is shown or promised.
- Yuki appears only in 9:6, in a sentence that fits all three forms. None of the drafts uses supperPot, the loft, the window, or a day count.
