fo_supper:8:4 | Hands are shown before the grace in this house, backs and then palms, as they were when Mei Lin was six. Her mother passes yours without remark. Mei Lin's have rope burns across both palms; these get a longer look, and a jar of salve set down by her bowl like a verdict. ¶ Mei Lin says the rope started it. Her mother says the rope is not at this table, and neither is that tone. You could tell them both what grows in a salve jar a whole household dips into, and are asked instead whether in your country they wash. {yuki}

fo_supper:8:4:y:great | Yuki sets one forepaw on the table's edge, where it takes up the room of three bowls. The mother inspects it gravely, between the toes as well, and passes it. *Thorough,* says Yuki. *I approve of her.*
fo_supper:8:4:y:human | Yuki, at the head of the table, presents both hands unasked, front and back. The mother bows over them without looking. "She did not check," you say. "She was being polite," says Yuki. "You were being inspected."
fo_supper:8:5 | Old Wen has told the house that you are a scholar, and the house has been thinking it over. Midway through the millet Mei Lin's mother lays down her chopsticks, squares them, and asks you which day is auspicious for the hanging of a gate. ¶ The timber is green and standing in wet clay. You know which rot will take it at the ground line, and in which year, and what a bucket of lime would do about that. You know nothing about days. "A dry one," you say. Mei Lin chokes on her tea and is looked at until she stops. The mother nods, once, as to a physician. {yuki}
fo_supper:8:5:y:cat | *She asked the mushroom man about the calendar,* Yuki observes, from the top of the rice chest. *Tell her about the rot.* She studies the mother's face. *No. Tell her when she has slept.*
fo_supper:8:5:y:great | Yuki, filling the floor between the table and the rice chest, opens one eye. *The mushroom man, consulted on the calendar. Mention the rot.* The eye goes to the mother, and closes. *Not tonight. She has had enough news.*
fo_supper:8:5:y:human | "He means that the clay should not be wet when the timber goes in," says Yuki, and takes more radish. The mother thanks her formally for the commentary. You had not meant that. It is better than what you meant.
fo_supper:8:6 | The coat was white once. Mei Lin's mother has ruled that it does not sit at her table in its present state, and it hangs on a peg, where it can think about the clay. Then she sees whose jacket it is. Her mother serves the millet in the usual order and takes no notice of either thing, at some cost. {yuki} You eat very carefully.

# notes
- 8:6 assumes the jacket is Mei Lin's late father's, kept by the mother (canon has her widowed; nothing says she kept his clothes). The text never names him; if that is too much, "a jacket from the chest" and cutting "Then she sees whose jacket it is" keeps the rest.
- 8:6 assumes James still has the lab coat in the Fallout and wears it to the wall.
- 8:5 assumes Old Wen calls James a scholar in Ashfall (the card gives "scholar" only as Ya's word for him), and that the palisade has a gate still to hang. "Not tonight" in the great form is a time word of the supper itself; swap for "Not yet" if the check objects.
- 8:4 and 8:5 use the rice chest, the salve jar and pickled radish as house furniture; none is in the given texts.
- 8:4 cat and 8:6 cat put Yuki on James's shoulder at table, per the card, not on the stove as the old texts do.
- "the mother" follows the old texts' usage; 8:4 opens with "Hands", 8:5 with "Old Wen", 8:6 with "The coat" (not "Your", in case the fold check matches "You" as a prefix).
