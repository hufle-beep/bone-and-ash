## What to look for in cultivation games, for Bone and Ash

- **Realms and breakthroughs:** how a breakthrough is prepared, gated, shown and failed (tribulations, bottlenecks, heart demons, pills, the chance of failure, what a failed breakthrough costs); how realm gaps are felt in fights and in how NPCs treat you (Bone and Ash's group e plans "realm flavour": how locals see each realm; James gets no tribulations and needs no herbs, an outlier, so look at what makes an outlier feel special).
- **Lifespan, death, reincarnation, rebirth, inheritance:** how a game carries things across lives (Bone and Ash's Coil keeps familiarity, Insights, Coil marks, knowledge; resets realm, skills, items, world flags). What carries over, how the player is told, what makes a new life feel different and not like a chore.
- **Time and the calendar:** months / years as the clock, events by date, deadlines, seclusion (closed-door cultivation) as a time skip, ageing.
- **NPCs and relationships:** sects, masters and disciples, Dao companions, rivals, grudges, favour, face; NPCs who live their own lives and cultivate on their own; how a relationship is shown; marriage and families (Bone and Ash: romance fades to black at the door, structure only).
- **Combat:** realm-gap combat, techniques and their Qi costs, artifacts, formations, talismans, enemy variety, how a fight is shown in text (group d: bare-hands brawls, sung-bone weapons, telegraphed moves, stances).
- **Crafting and economy:** alchemy, pills, refining, beast cores, spirit stones as currency (section 4: bone-work crafting, cores as currency).
- **Story and tone:** how text-heavy cultivation games write the world's gravity (the locals overly serious) and humour; random events; what players praise and complain about (reviews, NGA / TapTap / Steam threads): chores, grind, repetition, opaque systems.
- **Idle and incremental ones:** what the loop is, what automation looks like (section 4: Parallel Cognition threads need a design without a queue), offline progress, how prestige is themed as reincarnation.
