She asks it at the barley's corner, where the path narrows and her sleeve rides against yours the whole way round, a fact she leaves entirely unremarked. ¶ "We have them," you say. "They send letters." ¶ "Letters." She weighs it. "And you pay?" ¶ "Eventually. There are further letters first, each a little sterner." ¶ "Nobody walks up your road? Nobody takes a child?" ¶ "Nobody takes anything. There is a fine." ¶ She laughs before she can stop it, gets a cuff over her mouth, and looks along the lane for listeners. There are none. "A fine," she says, into the cloth. "I should like to be robbed by post." ¶ "It's slower. That is the whole of its charm."
mei_walk:5:5 | Out by the barley, Mei Lin admits that she once cried for a week at not being worth stealing. It comes out sideways. In the lane behind you a mother is rubbing soot beneath her children's eyes, as is done each autumn, so that they look sickly when heads are counted. ¶ "It fools nobody," Mei Lin says. Her shoulder is a finger's width from your arm and stays there. "Mine did it until I was seven. Then they tested me, found no root at all, and sent me home. I howled. I'd been told I was too precious to show them." ¶ "And the soot?" ¶ "She kept on with it two more years. In case they'd been wrong." ¶ "Thorough woman." ¶ "You've met her."

She has drifted in until her elbow keeps finding yours, and has not drifted out. {yuki} ¶ At the seventh you put your own hand down next to hers, not touching. ¶ She stares at it. mei_walk:6:5 | Somebody has turned the scarecrow round to face the road. Mei Lin stops you with a hand on your sleeve to look at it: the same straw, the same split hat, its back now to the barley it is kept to mind. ¶ "It's the custom," she says. "So that one of us is seen to be watching." Her hand stays where it is. "He's the only man in Ashfall who may stare at them. I have always thought he overdoes it." ¶ "He has the face for it."
mei_walk:6:6 | Between the old pear tree and the dyke run forty paces of path that no window in Ashfall can see, and Mei Lin has known it since she was fourteen. She tells you so with her arm drawn close through yours, walking slower at each step. ¶ "I worked it out for stealing pears," she says. "I want that understood." ¶ "Understood." ¶ "It was never any use for anything else." She leans her weight on you, a little, for exactly as far as the dyke, and straightens before the nearest roof comes into view. ¶ "Good pears?" ¶ "Sour," she says. "They always were."
# notes
- Yuki is in 5:6 only (one sentence at {yuki}, three forms); the other five leave her out.
- 5:5 has the collectors test Mei Lin at seven and find no root; her father's leaving at six (the supper) is not mentioned. Check against lore.md if her testing is fixed elsewhere.
- 5:6 names nine boundary stones and Old Wen touching them; 6:6 names a pear tree and a dyke by the field. All new furniture, none load-bearing; rename freely.
- 5:5 and 6:6 end on her line, 6:4 on narration, the rest on his, to vary the old shape.
- "a week" in 5:5 is her childhood crying, not a count of walks; change to "for days" if the time-word check objects.
