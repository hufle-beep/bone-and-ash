You copy her, and straighten to find her measuring the height at which you stopped. ¶ "Lower." ¶ "He's the tallest thing in Ashfall after the post," says Mei Lin. "They'll hang a banner on him." ¶ "He will stand behind the potter. The potter is broad." Her mother sits again, and turns to her daughter. "And you will stand by me, and your mouth will be shut." ¶ {yuki}

supper:6:2 | The Sect is not to be named at this table, by a rule older than your place at it, so Mei Lin has taken to calling it the weather. "The weather's hung its banners out," she says, passing the greens. "Red. I'd call that a sign of rain." ¶ "We do not discuss the weather at meals," says her mother. ¶ You remark, to be helpful, that it does seem to be settling in from the road. Mei Lin makes a noise into her millet. Her mother considers you, and decides the fault lies elsewhere. ¶ "The guest may speak of the weather. He does not know what he is saying." She serves you fish. "It hears, Mei Lin. It has always heard. Eat." ¶ {yuki}

supper:6:3 | Halfway down her bowl, Mei Lin's mother asks, of nobody, how old the weaver's girl is now. Mei Lin says seven. Her mother says the girl is small for seven, and Mei Lin says that she is a child and not a gourd, and is not graded by size. ¶ Nothing is said for a while. ¶ Then the mother takes the dish with what remains of the fish, covers it with a cloth and sets it by the door. Yuki regards the cloth with the look of one who was not consulted. ¶ "You will carry that to the weaver's when we have eaten. They have family visiting." ¶ "They haven't." ¶ "Then they will have fish. You will not go in. You will say it was left over." ¶ "Nothing has been left over in this house since I was born," says Mei Lin. ¶ "Say it as though you believed it."
# notes
- 6:3 assumes the collectors test children at the tribute in the year they turn seven (the card says Mei Lin was "tested at seven"), and that Ashfall has a weaver with a daughter. If either fails, the girl's age and trade can change without touching the rest.
- 6:1 assumes the village stands at the tribute post and bows when the collectors come, and reuses the potter (from the egg text) only as a broad man to stand behind.
- 6:2 never says "Sect" in dialogue, only in the narration's first sentence; "the weather" is Mei Lin's coinage here and could be picked up by texts 4-6 or left alone. The human Yuki line has the mother say "honoured one", as in the given texts.
- 6:1 and 6:2 use {yuki}; 6:3 has Yuki in one sentence that names no form.
- Word counts with the longest Yuki passage: about 146, 145, 146.
