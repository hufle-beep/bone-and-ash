fo_supper:10:1 | Millet cakes have appeared on the table in three stacks, pressed hard, wrapped in leaf and tied with grass. They are not supper. Mei Lin's mother has counted them twice and now counts them aloud, so that the ancestors may hear the provision was sufficient. ¶ Mei Lin says that a stack of that height will see her into old age, and that she will send word from there. Her voice does it nearly right. Her mother says that a traveller's food is not joked over, and moves the pickles nearer to her without looking up. ¶ Ya has followed the count with her head on one side. She takes the top cake off the nearest stack, unwraps it and eats it. "Food you carry, something takes," she explains, chewing. "Food you ate comes with you." She reaches for a second. ¶ The mother regards the short stack. Then she rises and puts more millet in to soak.
fo_supper:10:1:away | Supper. The mother counts out travel cakes and wraps each one twice.
fo_supper:10:2 | Straw sandals hang from the rafters in pairs, new, the plaiting still green. Mei Lin's mother made them to a number: she asked the hunters how long a pair lasts on root and stone, and multiplied. ¶ Mei Lin counts them and says she had not known she owned so many feet. She says it lightly and then goes on looking up; nobody plaits that many for a short walk. Her mother tells her to eat. ¶ A pair lies by Ya's bowl as well, since a guide is of the party and the party is shod. Ya smells one, compares it with her own sole, which is much the same article, and hangs the pair round her neck. "Hers go in three days, in the wet," she tells the cook, kindly. "I carry her after." ¶ The mother thanks her for the information and asks how many days of wet there are. Ya begins on her fingers, and runs out.
fo_supper:10:2:away | Supper. New straw sandals hang from the rafters, and nobody mentions them.
fo_supper:10:3 | Mei Lin's basket is on the table where the pickles ought to be, and her mother is unpacking it. Each thing comes out, is looked at, and is laid in one of two rows. Nobody has said what the rows are. Both combs went left. ¶ Mei Lin says she is being robbed in her own house by her own blood and wants it witnessed. You, the witness, eat your millet. When the hair oil goes left her laugh arrives a beat behind itself. ¶ Ya leans over unasked and moves three more things left: a sash, a bronze mirror, a pot of lip-red. The mother watches the hand do it, and lets it. Then Ya lifts the roll of bandage out of the right-hand row. "This much," she says, offended. "She thinks I guide badly." ¶ "I think my daughter climbs," says the mother, and puts it back. Yuki has an opinion of both rows and is keeping it.
fo_supper:10:3:away | Supper. Mei Lin's basket stands packed by the wall; her mother serves round it.
# notes
- Motifs used, for the 4-6 run to steer clear of: (1) travel cakes counted aloud, Ya eats the provisions; (2) straw sandals plaited to a number, Ya hangs her pair on the necklace; (3) the basket unpacked into two rows, the bandage roll.
- Assumed: the hunters know roughly how long sandals last in the Blackroot; Mei Lin owns a bronze mirror, hair oil and lip-red (swap for any small vanities canon allows); the mother addresses Ya directly and Ya answers "the cook".
- 10:2 ends on Ya running out of fingers: it gives no length for the trip, but if canon fixes the walk at under ten days, cut the last sentence to "Ya begins on her fingers."
- The away lines assume a going is still known to the household when Ya has not been met; if that path has no journey yet, 10:1:away and 10:3:away need a neutral line.
- Yuki appears only in 10:3, in a sentence that fits all forms.
