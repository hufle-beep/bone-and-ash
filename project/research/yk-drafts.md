### Q-sister
Yuki leads you off until nobody is near enough to hear, and changes there. No ceremony: one step she is a cat, the next she is standing, a head shorter than you, shaking her sleeves straight like someone back in a coat she left on a hook.

"There," she says. "Hands again. You know what comes with the hands."

"The question."

"The question." She looks up at you, ears pricked. "You called me your sister, last time round, and I was a very good one. I have references, though none of them remember. That answer was yours to give then and it's yours to give now, and the two needn't match. I'll put it to you every time the world hands me a mouth. It costs me nothing, and I like to keep current." Her tail curls once, unhurried. "So. Family, James? Or more?"

### Q-lover
She waits until no one is close enough to overhear, then stands up out of the cat's shape as easily as out of a chair. She tugs her collar level and counts her fingers. All present.

"Before you start," Yuki says aloud, "I remember. Every bit. So do you, and you've been careful not to look at me about it, which I noticed and found sweet."

"I wasn't sure it carried over."

"The memory does. The answer doesn't. I won't hold you to a thing you said in a world that has since been put back in its box." One ear tilts, dry. "You get a clean go whenever I get a face. Say it over, say otherwise; you couldn't lose me with a map. And you'll get the clean go after this one too. What am I to you, this turn? Family, or more?"

### Q-wait
Yuki picks a spot out of everyone's hearing and takes her human shape there, quick and tidy, a thing practised. She rolls her shoulders and checks that she has thumbs.

"We stopped at 'not yet'," she says. "Then it all began again, and it has taken me until now to grow a voice back. I'm not complaining. I'm explaining the delay."

"I never did answer you."

"No. You weren't ready, and then there was no more of it left to be ready in. That isn't a debt, and I haven't been keeping interest on it." She clasps her hands behind her back; her tail sways, slow. "I ask whenever I've two feet to stand on while I do it. This is that and nothing heavier. Whatever you tell me, I stay, and the asking comes round again regardless. So, James. Am I family to you? Or am I more?"

### F
The fire has reached the stage where it wants watching and not feeding. Across it the white shape gets up, stretches, and is a woman by the end of the stretch: kneeling, sleeves neat, hair already everywhere.

"You've been expecting this," Yuki says.

"Since the breakthrough."

"Then I can skip the preamble. You've heard it, and I was better at it then." She holds her palms to the heat, pleased with them. "I know what you told me before. I've kept it, and I shan't quote it at you. People change between one turn of this and the next. You more than most; I've watched." An ear flicks. "So I ask fresh each time I can speak, and I'll go on doing it for as long as the turns do. No answer you give could cost you me." She looks at you over the flames. "Which is it, James? Family, or more?"

### A-sister-same
"Sister," Yuki says, tasting it. "Again. Good. I've got the hang of that one."

She comes over and leans her whole weight on your arm, as the cat has always done and with far more leverage.

"The terms stand as they were. No amendments, except that you chew too loudly and I've had no means of saying so until I had lips."

"You've said so through the bond for forty years."

"Aloud is different. Aloud counts." Her tail thumps your shin once, settled. "The same, then. It suits us. And when I ask you next, don't read anything into it. I only like hearing you say it."

### A-sister-changed
Yuki's ears go back, come forward, and stay forward. She nods.

"Family," she says. "Right. Then let it be known that I was an excellent lover, and that I retire undefeated."

"You were. It isn't that—"

"I know what it isn't. I live in your head, James. I felt you choose, and you chose kindly." She steps in and leans her shoulder into your ribs, hard enough to register. "I was your kin before either of us could walk. It was never the consolation prize. It's what all the rest stands on." She straightens your lapel, proprietary. "And I get to be rude about your taste in women again, with a clear conscience. I've missed that."

### A-lover-same
Her fingers find the gaps between yours without looking, having been there before.

"There you are," Yuki says, low.

She goes up on her toes. The kiss is brief and exact, a signature on something already agreed, and afterwards she stays close enough that you feel her breathe out.

"I did wonder if you'd say it twice," she says. "I'd have been fine. I want that understood. I am simply more fine at present."

"Understood."

"Good." She turns, fits herself under your arm and rests her head against your shoulder, your hand kept firmly in hers. "The rest keeps. It kept through the world ending. It can keep a little longer."

### A-lover-changed
Yuki looks at your hand, then at you, and her tail stops moving altogether.

"That's not what you said before," she says.

"No."

"I'm not objecting. I'm making sure you know." She takes the hand with care, turning it over as if it might be a different one. Then she steps close, lays her free palm flat on your chest, and kisses you once, lightly: the corner of your mouth, then the middle of it.

"Hm," says Yuki. Her ears have tipped towards you and won't be talked out of it. "I was a good sister. I intend to be insufferable at this." She settles into your side, fingers laced through yours. "No rush on what's left. I've only just arrived."

### Q-wait alt
Yuki walks you out of earshot first, then changes: a shrug, and she is on two feet, smoothing her skirt with hands she plainly approves of.

"You owe me a sentence," she says. "Don't make that face. I'm not collecting. I'm telling you I still have the place marked."

"It got cut short."

"Everything did. I don't count that against you; you can't be late for a thing the world took away." Her ears are easy, her tail loose. "I ask once per mouth, as a policy. There's no clock on it and no wrong reply, and when all this winds back you'll hear it from me again, just as mildly." She tips her head. "So. What am I to you, James? Family? Or more?"
