roof:3:3 | Mei Lin teaches you to read the weather off the village, since you admitted that at home a voice in the wall simply told you. ¶ "Start with the smoke. If it lies down along the roofs, rain. If it stands, frost." She points with her chin. "The miller's knee is good for two days out, but he charges." ¶ "Charges what?" you ask, and she says, "You have to listen to the rest of the miller." ¶ You ask your arm, quietly. It offers a guess for a sky it has never met, and you can tell it is guessing. "What does it say?" she asks. ¶ "That it doesn't know. It's quite upset about it." ¶ "Then it is more honest than the miller." She wets a finger, holds it up, and takes your wrist to hold yours up beside it. "East. Rain by morning. Say it as if you knew."
roof:3:3:stay | The cold arrives from the east, as forecast, and she takes the credit for it on every rung. Indoors she makes you say which way the smoke lay. You get it right, and she nods as her mother nods at a clean floor. The herb loft receives a man qualified in one wind.
roof:3:4 | After dark Ashfall goes on talking, and Mei Lin lies back on the thatch and tells you who everyone is by ear. ¶ "That is the cooper's door. He oils everything but the hinge." A cough, two roofs over. "Old Wen. He is awake and wants it known." ¶ A dog begins, and a second dog takes the matter up. Somewhere a loom is still going. "She should be asleep," Mei Lin says, and does not say why she isn't. ¶ "And that?" A low, even sawing has started in the house beneath you. ¶ "That is nothing." She is quite firm. "My mother does not snore. Half the village has not heard it for years." ¶ You listen to nothing together until it turns over and settles.
roof:3:4:stay | The loom is still going when the thatch gives up its warmth. Inside she walks on the edges of the boards, where they keep quiet, and shows you which ones with her toe. The nothing behind her mother's door does not break rhythm. You reach the herb loft having made no sound she will have to explain.
roof:3:5 | The game is called Magistrate, it is Mei Lin's invention as of this evening, and you are losing it. ¶ One of you charges a roof in the lane with an offence. The other must defend it. "The potter's chimney," she says. "Leaning with intent." ¶ "It's deferring to the wind. Respect for elders." ¶ "The wind is not his elder." She weighs it. "Acquitted. Narrowly. Your turn." ¶ You charge the hen-house opposite with keeping irregular hours. She defends it at length, citing a fox, a precedent and the character of the hens, and you are obliged to let it go. "And this roof?" you ask. "Two persons on it, after supper." ¶ "That one I prosecute myself." She pulls the blanket up to her chin. "Slowly. It could take years."
roof:3:5:stay | The court rises when neither of its officers can feel their feet. She hears one more case on the ladder, yours, for treading on the judge's hand, and fines you an apology, paid at once. In the house she bows to you as one magistrate to another. You take the verdict up to the herb loft and sleep on it.
roof:3:6 | Mei Lin tells you a thing she has never told anyone, and makes you swear on the roof ridge before she does. ¶ "When I was nine I ate the honey cake off the house shrine. The one for the ancestors." She keeps her eyes on the lane as she says it, in case it hears. "I said a rat had it. My mother set traps for a month." ¶ "Did they catch anything?" ¶ "A rat. An innocent one." She pulls her knees up. "I still leave a crumb by the wall at festivals. For his family." You ask whether that is the whole crime. ¶ "It is quite enough. They were my ancestors." She glances at you sideways. "Do not make that face. The one that says it is small." ¶ "It's enormous. I'm barely holding the roof up." She knocks her shoulder against yours, hard, and leaves it there.
roof:3:6:stay | She swears you in afresh on the ladder, in case the roof ridge was not binding. Passing the house shrine she does not look at it, and it, you notice, has the tact not to look at her. You climb to the herb loft carrying a dead rat's good name, and you mean to keep it.
roof:3:7 | Neither of you says much on the roof tonight, and Mei Lin lets the quiet stand. ¶ The wine is poured and mostly forgotten. Wind goes through the millet stubble below the village with a sound of someone turning pages. From the square comes the knock of baskets being stacked by lantern, and neither of you turns to look. ¶ She has brought some mending up and does none of it. After a while she puts it aside and lays her hand on the thatch between you, palm up, as if she had only set it there to dry. ¶ You take it. Her fingers are cold and close hard. "You are thinking," she says. ¶ "Hardly at all." ¶ "Good. Keep doing that."
roof:3:7:stay | The cold comes for your feet and then for the rest of you. She lets go of your hand at the ladder only because the ladder insists, and takes it back at the bottom, until the loft's own ladder parts you. "Goodnight" is all either of you says. In the herb loft your fingers go on being held a while longer.

- roof:3:3: assumes his home had a house voice that gave the weather ("a voice in the wall"), and that the omnitool can be asked and can fail without speaking aloud; the miller's knee is invented. "Rain by morning" is her forecast, not a promise of a later scene; cut that sentence to "East. Rain. Say it as if you knew." if the time-word check objects.
- roof:3:4: a cooper and a night loom are invented villagers; the loom is the only nod to the tribute (someone weaving after dark). The mother snoring is invented.
- roof:3:5: a potter and a hen-house opposite are invented. "It could take years" is her joke, not a promise; cut the last two words' sentence if it reads as foreshadowing.
- roof:3:6: assumes a house shrine with ancestor offerings in her mother's house; "nine" and "a month" are story numbers, not gains. Kept clear of her father and of the root test (the other run's ground).
- roof:3:7: baskets stacked in the square stand for the tribute without naming it; "tonight" is the only time word. Mending is invented.
