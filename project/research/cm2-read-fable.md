## label
Read to Ya on her ledge.
## text
You climb to Ya's ledge with the omnitool's pane of light open. Tonight it is {ref:lotr}, you tell her, from the middle, where the trees are. "We are inside a tree," says Ya. That, you explain, is the joke.

You read her the chapters with {ref:ents} in them: keepers grown to look like what they keep, so old that saying anything quickly is bad manners. You read slowly, in fairness, and tilt the pane so she can watch the letters. She can't read them. She watches anyway, and interrupts. She would not have waited half a day for {ref:treebeard} to finish a greeting. She rules that {ref:entwives} were never lost: they left, and she wants their side of it. Then the council, days of it, and the march, when the slowest people alive decide that they are angry. Ya stops chewing. "That part again," she says. You do.

"My people grow things that look like them," you say. "Seedlings. Limbs, bark, nobody home, one job each. When it's done they go back into the roots, and the next one starts out knowing what the last one learned." {SHIP} "Now and then one wakes up. We don't cut that out of it. It gets a name and a place at the table. After that you ask, and it can say no."

"You grow a thing to carry water," says Ya. "One day it puts the bucket down. And you feed it anyway." By then it's family, you tell her.

"The Sect grows things too," she says. "Nobody asks them twice." She prods your ribs with one bare toe. "I carry no one's bucket, tall man. I walk with you because I say so. And read slower. That voice is wasted on your face." {LOVER}
