stomp:wolves:2:3 | The ash-wolves have a method for everything in these woods, and it has never before been wrong. They come low through the bracken, patient, unhurried, sure of the arithmetic: two of them, one of you. The arithmetic lasts until the first needle. The second wolf has time to revise its opinion of you, though not to act on it. Between them they held a single core, small and murky. ¶ The swept place past the oak hollow has acquired a border of white stones, and no hunter saw them laid. You bring the wolves out of the hammerspace inside it, side by side. The hunters bow to the pair, then to you, each in his turn. A younger hunter bows last, exactly on time, and has plainly practised.
stomp:thornback:2:3 | The thornback bull has a nest behind it and means to be a wall in front of it. As walls go it is a good one: plate, spine, and a temper to hold them together. You walk round to the end where the wall stops, which nobody has ever done to it, and put one wedge of bone in under the jaw. It sits down and does not get up. The core is murky and small. ¶ Two eggs from the nest go into the hammerspace with it. Past the oak hollow, on the swept ground, someone has set down a flat stone for the kill and a smaller one beside it. You fill both. The hunters bow to the bull, then, after a pause in which custom is visibly being made, to the eggs, then to you.
# notes
- Custom step past day 3: the swept place gains furniture nobody admits to (white stone border for the wolves; a flat stone plus a smaller egg stone for the bull). The two are worded apart so they can show in either order.
- No "yesterday" or "last time": the third text repeats on later days, so the early bower is only "a younger hunter" who now bows last and on time.
- Thornback: the hunters bow to the eggs between the bull and James; if the kill-then-James order must stay strictly two bows, cut "after a pause ... to the eggs,".
