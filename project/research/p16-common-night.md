## The scene
`night`, the forest's night before sleep, in the prologue: the Blackroot Wilds (black bark, grey moss, a bruise-coloured sky), a camp on a dry rise above a stream, a fallen trunk to put his back against. James and Yuki are alone: no people, no village yet, no Mei Lin, no Ya. The text shows after "Night falls. Settle in." (about 21:00); the choices under it are "Leave a thread on watch and sleep." and "Just sleep. You've earned it."; the next screen is the sleep and the morning. So each text is the dark arriving and him settling for the night.

On the long calendar the forest lasts three weeks (21 nights). The scene has fifteen texts now (in the file: a `byDay` list, one text a night in order; nights 16 and 17 now read texts 1 and 2 again, and nights 18–21 texts 3–6, folded). This push adds six, read on nights 16–21 (the end of the forest stay, though nobody in the text knows it: the camp is long his, the forest still is not). Each stands alone.

Every text keeps the old texts' shape, three paragraphs:
1. the dark arriving, and one thing of this world's night (about 40–60 words);
2. ONE sentence split by the fire (`fire`: there is a fire; `nofire`: there is none; each 10–25 words), then ONE sentence of Yuki by form (`y:cat` the small white cat, in his lap or on him; `y:great` the great root-kin, tiger-sized, lying round him; `y:human` the woman with cat ears and tail, sitting against his side; each 10–25 words; bond-speech in italics `*like this*` for cat and great, spoken aloud in quotes for the woman, if she speaks at all);
3. the watch-thread: one of his nine threads takes the watch, in a new way (15–35 words).

Rules:
- **The fold:** a text read before folds to its FIRST PARAGRAPH with a "Read it all again" link: it must stand alone as a reminder of that night, and not open with the same first two words as any text in the file, nor with "Night", "The night", "The dark", "Dusk", "Tonight" or "You".
- The forest has no dusk: the dark arrives, it does not fade in. No "dusk", "twilight", "sunset", "evening".
- No counts ("the sixteenth night", "a week now", "for days"), no "first", "last", "again" about nights, no promises about a day; no clock times, "midnight", "dawn", "tomorrow".
- Nothing happens: nothing attacks, nothing comes near the rise, no tracks, nothing that foreshadows a later night. Something may be heard far off.
