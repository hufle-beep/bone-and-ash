## reflect
### text
You have the archive open before you have decided to, which appears to be how evenings go here. Out of all of it you pick {ref:reflect}, and keep it low: bright, quick, electronic, most of it in Japanese, and about as suited to this forest as a party hat on a headstone. The black trees take it without comment.

It is a song for two, sung by one: a singer and her other self, the one behind the face, who wants no part of her, and without whom, she owns up near the end, she would never have got this far. You have heard it some hundreds of times. Until tonight you had not thought of it as being about anyone you knew.

Yuki's ears come round from the dark to the omnitool, and stay there. {YUKI}

You play it again. Out in the dark, nothing objects.

## hammerdeep
### text
Whichever it is to be, the morning needs something under it. You thumb the omnitool and put on {ref:hammerdeep}, sung by {ref:clamavi}. It rolls out over the tea, low and many-voiced, like stone being moved: a fortress-home praised for the mighty hands that made it, the craftsman's honest work first and the ale after, and the ones who died keeping their lands.

Old Wen hears it to the end with his cup held level. In the doorway, two women with water yokes have given up pretending to be on their way somewhere. "Your ancestors," he says. It isn't a question.

You explain that they are dwarves, that dwarves are short, bearded and made up, and that the fortress is made up as well.

He weighs this with his whole face. "Then somebody made them a home worth dying for, and troubled to sing that the work comes before the cup," he says. "The hunters will hear it before one stake is lifted. Play it at the well."

## genesis
### text
A silence that complete wants something done to it. You turn the omnitool up, which you never do, and put on {ref:genesis}: several voices at once, brash, giving notice that they have begun and intend to be trouble, with a twist on the way that they are plainly pleased about. In your archive it belongs to five who broke out of a prison. You make it four, Yuki included, and nobody has called Ashfall a prison in your hearing.

"Louder," Ya calls, from wherever she has got to. "Let everything in there know we're coming. Saves me the walk."

Mei Lin keeps her eyes on the black trunks. "My mother can hear that."

"Yes."

"Good," she says, not quite steadily. "Don't turn it down."

You don't. Behind you nobody straightens. Ashfall holds its bow to the back of a man walking off under the worst music it has ever heard, and goes on holding it until the forest has all four of you, and the noise as well.

## boof_pack
### text
A first camp under trees this size calls for something with dignity, so you put on {ref:boof_pack}. An easy, loafing beat; a saxophone with nowhere to be; and {ref:aethelstan}, explaining at some length that he can't come out this evening, because he is staying in with the boys.

Ya asks what he is saying. You tell her. She laughs until she has to put her spear down, makes you play it over, and joins the horn, loudly, on a note of her own. "He's right," she says. "Good fire, your own people, and some fool wants you to go *out*? I'd hunt with him."

Mei Lin has gone very still. "Somebody made that deliberately," she says. "And your people kept it. And out of everything you might have carried here, you carried that." The saxophone comes round again and she puts both hands over her ears. "It sounds like a goose being consoled."

Yuki has the look of someone watching a cart roll slowly into a pond. {YUKI}

## waters_call
### text
You choose by title, which you would admit is not a method. Low, so that it stays on the raft, you play {ref:waters_call}. She closed her streams with it: the last thing of the night, about five minutes long, and then everybody went home. How it sounds anywhere else you couldn't swear to. Here, over water that keeps a copy of everything, it sounds like being told, kindly, that it is late.

"If they come up to listen," Ya says, hardly moving her mouth, "you do the explaining, scholar." She doesn't tell you to stop.

Mei Lin asks what it is for. You tell her: for the end of things, for sending people home. She looks down at the black surface, and at whoever is under it. "Then let it finish," she says.

It finishes. The dripping takes up its post again. Yuki, who has watched the reflections throughout, reports no change, in the voice of someone who had been ready for one.

## lyfjaberg
### text
Since nothing anybody says is going to help, you stop saying things and put on {ref:lyfjaberg}, by {ref:wardruna}. Goat-horn, lyre, and drums pitched so deep that the ash seems to settle to them. Its title is an old word for healing-mountain: in the poem, the sick who climb it are cured. The people who made the song add that a hard mountain is climbed with the mind as much as the body, which is the kind of remark people make from the top.

Mei Lin, with a herb-gatherer's suspicion of rivals, asks whether it works. "The poem is confident," you say. "Poems are," she says. But her feet have found the drum, and she takes the first black slope in time with it.

"If I get cured of anything up there," says Ya, "I want it back." She goes up ahead of the beat, to show it who is leading.

## trollabundin
### text
The hunt was over when the bait came down. What is left is a fight, and for a fight you have music. You wake the omnitool and put on {ref:trollabundin}: one frame drum, and a woman's voice that chants, and breathes, and rises. Its title means bewitched; bound by trolls. Your pulse finds the drum and stays on it, and everything that is not the ape goes quiet and very far off.

"*Now* he plays it," says Ya, and laughs like a knife coming out.

## music_box
### text
Nobody sleeps. You can hear everyone not doing it. You raise your forearm and open the omnitool's pane, a square of light with a drawn singer in it, and pick {ref:music_box}. An orchestra comes up among the roots. Then the voice arrives on top of it and climbs, and goes on climbing, to where a voice ought to need a rope, and is at home there. In the gap in the trunk the other song carries on underneath, and doesn't interrupt.

"That is {ref:ironmouse}," you say. "The face is a drawing. She trained for the opera. Then her body stopped making what fights off sickness, nearly all of it. She lives in one room, mostly in bed. Her lungs took an infection; now she breathes bottled air, day and night. Every week she needs the part of the blood she can't make, and strangers give it. So she sang from the bed, behind the drawing, and few people have a bigger crowd. She asks the crowd to give blood. Singing costs her. She says none of it is for show."

Mei Lin has gone quiet, as she does at a sickbed. "Nothing in her to fight with," she says. "A cough could finish her, and she spends her breath on *that*." She wipes her face with the heel of her hand, as if it were a patient's. "I have given up on people who had more left. I'm going to stop. Play the end again."

Ya is up on one elbow, glaring at the pane as at a rival. "Can't stand. Can't breathe. Can't go out her own door. And she does *that*, lying down." She thumps her breastbone. "That's the strong. Not the arm. Any ox has the arm." "If I whine in that tree, scholar, hit me."

You play the end again. Yuki, who has heard it more often than anyone here, listens as though she hadn't.

## durin
### text
You have a song for this. You have carried it most of your life without a room that deserved it. You set the omnitool to {ref:durin} and, against habit, turn it up.

Slow voices, deep, from a long way down: a king who woke when the world was young and gave the hills their names, and his halls in their glory; then the turn, the world gone grey, the halls dark. The hollow takes each line and hands it back off every gallery a breath late, so that the dead wood sings bass.

Mei Lin bows her head, as at a burial done properly at last. "Who were they?" she asks, when it ends.

"Dwarves. Short, stubborn. Somebody made them up."

"Then somebody made them up very well," she says, and dries her eyes on her sleeve, annoyed with both of you.

"Beards to the belt, I'd bet, and they hit things," Ya says. "Finally. Somebody in here I'd drink with."

## mumei_song
### text
The minute needs something in it. Your hand goes to your forearm without consulting you and puts on {ref:mumei_song}, low, against the hull.

It is sung in a language nobody down here has. It is about being nameless and forgotten, and living on regardless, nameless still, for someone somewhere. In front of you is a bow with the foot of one letter on it. Somebody waited for this ship. Then, at some point, somebody stopped.

"What is she saying?" Mei Lin asks.

"That she hasn't got a name, and is staying anyway."

Mei Lin looks at the buried bow, and at you, and has the tact to say nothing, which costs her.

"So it's got no name," Ya says. "It's got us stood here. Most graves get less." From Ya, this is condolence.

Yuki watches the bow and not you, which is how she gives you the minute.

## non_fiction
### text
When the drummers finish you put on {ref:non_fiction}. You pick it because this is the last fire, and because by this hour tomorrow Ashfall will be telling the journey wrong, and you would like it on record how it sounded from the people who were in it. Five voices, about a myth of their own: one they wrote together, side by side, and are plainly pleased with.

You tell them that much. Ya counts round the fire on her fingers and comes up short whichever end she starts from. "Five," she says. "We're one down."

"The seed," says Mei Lin. "Five."

"A seed can't sing."

"*You* can't sing."

"I sing like a boar," Ya says, with pride.

"That is what I said."

## breathe
### text
The street is long, and nobody on it means to speak first. You put on {ref:breathe}, out of {ref:in_the_heights}, as {ref:ironmouse} sang it once on a stream, low, at walking pace. A young woman comes home from her first year at a far school with her whole neighbourhood's hopes on her back, and is afraid she has dropped them. You were not carrying this street's hopes when you left. It turns out somebody put them in the pack anyway.

"That's the bed-woman," Ya says, pleased, far too loud. "She's good at scared. Makes it sound like a fight."

Mei Lin has none of the words and hears it regardless. Her eyes find one doorway, and her chin comes up. In the doorway her mother, arms folded, hears a girl's voice come out of the outlander's sleeve and gives it the look she keeps for unwashed herbs.

The grandmother with the dusty forehead bows to your forearm as well, to be thorough. Yuki accepts the silence of Ashfall as her due.

## dwarven_forge
### text
You don't answer at once. A question of that size deserves accompaniment. Your hand goes to your forearm and picks {ref:dwarven_forge}, and you leave it loud enough for the road.

Deep voices come up out of the omnitool like men out of a mine, singing of the hammer and the fire in the time a hammer keeps: people who made what they needed and asked nobody's leave. The sweet rot in the air does not thin. It does get company.

"He answers the Elder with *smiths*," one of the crimson twelve whispers, appalled. Nobody corrects him. It is close enough.
