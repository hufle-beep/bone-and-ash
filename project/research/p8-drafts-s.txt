supper:4:4 | You stand your chopsticks upright in the millet while you reach for the greens, and the table stops. Mei Lin's mother takes them out and lays them flat across your bowl, without hurry. "Upright is for the dead," she says. "They are fed at the shrine shelf, on their days. Not here, and not by guests." You apologise. She accepts it with a nod the size of a grain. ¶ "He only wanted the greens and he's buried the lot of us," says Mei Lin. "Efficient." ¶ "You will fetch the water tomorrow," her mother tells her. "Both pails." ¶ Mei Lin opens her mouth. ¶ "And the morning after." ¶ Mei Lin shuts it and eats her fish, studying you over the bowl's rim with no remorse whatsoever.
supper:4:5 | The cooper's wife comes to the door in the middle of supper to return a sieve, and to look at you. Mei Lin's mother meets her on the threshold and does not move from it. The sieve was borrowed at midsummer. "You are prompt," the mother says. The cooper's wife leans left. The mother is somehow also to the left. {yuki} ¶ "She'll tell the lane you have four arms," says Mei Lin, once the door is shut. ¶ "She will tell them what she saw. A man eating millet." Her mother sits. "With his elbows on my table." You remove them.

supper:4:6 | Mei Lin's mother asks for your glasses, across the fish, with her palm held out flat. You hand them over. She holds them up to the lamp, then looks through them at the wall, at her daughter and at you, in that order. "They make the room worse," she says. ¶ You explain that your eyes are the trouble and the glass corrects them. She considers a man who carries his cure on his nose, and holds them out again, folded. ¶ Mei Lin intercepts them and puts them on. "Oh," she says, peering about, delighted. "No wonder you bow to the doorpost." ¶ "A guest's eyes are not a toy." Her mother does not raise her voice. Mei Lin returns them by the stems, with enormous care.
supper:5:4 | The potter's hen has laid in this house's woodpile, and Mei Lin's mother brings the egg to the table and sets it before you. "You have no cousins in Ashfall," she says. "Whose is it?" ¶ The egg keeps its own counsel. You say that the hen is the potter's, the woodpile is hers, and that among your people the egg would go back with a note about the hen. ¶ "A hen cannot be written to." ¶ "The note is for the potter." ¶ "He means a rude note," says Mei Lin. "Let me write it." ¶ "You will carry the egg back, and you will say good evening, and nothing else." Her mother turns to you again. "It was the right answer. It took too long."
supper:5:5 | For as long as you have eaten here you have been asking for the millet in the wrong tone, and tonight Mei Lin tells you what you have been saying instead. "Pass the sorrow," she says, in your accent, which she does well. "Is there any sorrow left in the pot." She has to put her bowl down. ¶ "You knew," says her mother, "and you let a guest of this house go on saying it." Mei Lin stops laughing, mostly. ¶ Then the mother turns to face you and says the word. You say it back. She says it again. On the fifth attempt she nods and passes you the millet, and Mei Lin tells her fish, very quietly, that she will miss the sorrow.
supper:5:6 | After the fish Mei Lin's mother brings down the dried persimmons, which hang in the rafters to be eaten at the new year and not before. She cuts the string and puts two by each bowl. Mei Lin looks at the persimmons, then at her mother, and for once has nothing to say. ¶ "The mice would have had them," the mother says. ¶ There has not been a mouse in this house since Yuki moved in. Nobody mentions that either. Yours is dense and dark and tastes of a better month than this one. ¶ "Next year's will be sweeter," the mother says, to the string in her hand. "The tree was young." She winds the string and puts it in her sleeve, because string is string.
# notes
- Yuki is used once only (supper:4:5, three forms); supper:5:6 names her in a clause that reads the same in every form, so it has no {yuki} slot.
- supper:4:4 leans on the upright-chopsticks custom as an Ashfall rule (the dead are fed at a shrine shelf); if lore has no shrine shelf, "at the graves" fits the same line.
- supper:5:5 assumes the local speech is tonal and James's is accented; if he is meant to speak it without fault, this one needs swapping.
- supper:5:6 is the "tribute is closer, nobody says so" text: the new-year food opened early. It names no season and no day.
- supper:5:4 uses the potter, not the widow or the dyer, to stay clear of the roof's lamp bet.
