roof:1:4 | Mei Lin asks, with full ceremony, whether she may look through your glasses. ¶ You hand them over. She holds them by the very edges, like a thing on loan from a shrine, and puts them on. The lane swims. She looks at the well, the roof ridge, her own hand. "Your country is soft at the edges," she says. ¶ "That's my eyes. The country was sharp enough." You tap the frame. "These correct the fault." ¶ "And nobody mended the eyes themselves?" She sounds shocked on your behalf. ¶ "It was offered. I'd grown used to the furniture." ¶ She gives them back and watches you settle them on your nose. "My mother says you read people like small print. I shall tell her it is a medical matter."
roof:1:4:stay | You go down the ladder with the glasses in your pocket, to prove a point nobody raised, and miss the bottom rung. She catches your elbow in both hands and lets go at once, as propriety requires. "Small print," she says, quite kindly. You find the herb loft by feel, and put the glasses on to look at the dark.
roof:1:5 | Mei Lin asks, as politely as it can be asked, who died. ¶ "Nobody. Why?" ¶ "The coat." She nods at it. "White is for funerals. You have worn mourning since you came here, and Ashfall is too well brought up to ask for whom." ¶ "It's a work coat. Where I'm from it means you are allowed near the glassware." ¶ She receives this with the gravity it deserves, and looks out over the dark fields while she rearranges the village. "Then the miller's wife has wasted her respect. She has been giving you half the lane, for your loss." ¶ "I assumed she disliked me," you say, and Mei Lin agrees that she does. "But tenderly," she says, "until now."
roof:1:5:stay | She is shivering before she will admit it, so you put the coat round her, since it is there. She descends in a stranger's mourning, sleeves well past her hands, and returns it at the bottom folded square, with formal thanks for the loan of your dead. You take them up to the herb loft with you.
roof:2:7 | Mei Lin falls asleep in the middle of an argument she was winning. ¶ It concerned whether a mushroom is a plant. She held that anything picked into a basket is a plant, and that your years of study had been spent avoiding the fact. Then her head came down on your shoulder, mid-clause, with the verdict undelivered. ¶ Your arm goes numb. Nine threads of thought convene on the question of moving it and vote against, unanimously, which they have not managed on any other subject. ¶ A dog in the lane barks at nothing and is satisfied. She wakes all at once, sits up very straight, and sees to her hairpin. ¶ "I was resting my eyes," she says. "And it is a plant."
roof:2:7:stay | You concede the mushroom on the ladder, your arm being in no state to argue; it hangs off you like borrowed goods. She watches you fumble a rung and says nothing, which from her is an apology. Outside the herb loft she adds that a toadstool is a plant as well, and is gone before you can appeal.
roof:3:1 | Mei Lin comes up the ladder with her hands stained green to the wrist, and holds them clear of the blanket. ¶ "Ash-grass," she says. "It does not wash out. It wears out." She shows you the palms like a ledger. "Old Wen says you can tell a gatherer's season by her hands. Mine say I have been busy, and that nobody should ask why." ¶ You ask instead what the plant is good for. ¶ "Fevers. Boils. Bad temper in oxen." She thinks about it. "Nothing in the person who picks it." ¶ You take one hand and turn it to the starlight, professionally. The stain stops in a neat line where her sleeve was tied. ¶ "Chlorophyll," you report. "Very healthy." You do not give the hand back at once, and she does not send for it.
roof:3:1:stay | The cold finds her hands before the rest of her. She takes the rungs with her fingertips, to spare the wood, and leaves a green thumbprint on your cuff anyway, which she studies and does not apologise for. You carry it into the herb loft, where water makes no impression on it either.
roof:3:2 | There is a hollow worn in the thatch below the ridge, and Mei Lin tells you whose it was. ¶ "My father's. He had a spirit root, a thin one, and he came up here to cultivate it. He said the Qi was cleaner above the smoke." She tucks her feet under the blanket. "Is it?" ¶ You let your senses out over the roof and bring them back. "The same as in the yard." ¶ "I thought so." She is not disappointed. "He wanted the view, and a reason my mother would accept. He never climbed down any stronger. Only calmer." ¶ "That's most of what it's for." ¶ "Then he was a great master, and the Sect missed it." She pats the hollow you are sitting in. "Mind his place. It took him years."
roof:3:2:stay | When the cold comes she smooths the hollow flat with her palm, as one does a bed, and goes down ahead of you. On the ladder she says he snored up there, and the whole lane agreed to call it cultivation. You reach the herb loft feeling calmer and no stronger, which she would say runs in the roof.
# notes
- roof:1:5 assumes white is the mourning colour in Ashfall, and names a "miller's wife" (the miller is in the game; his wife is new).
- roof:1:4 assumes James's people could have mended his eyes and he declined.
- roof:3:1 invents "ash-grass" as the herb she has been cutting (the tribute's grass is implied, never named); swap for the game's own name if there is one.
- roof:3:2 assumes her father cultivated on the roof and is dead (her mother a widow); it says nothing of how he died or of the Sect taking him. "The Sect missed it" may want checking against lore.md.
- roof:2:7 leans on a shoulder (allowed by the brief); the shared text names no Yuki form because she is not in any of the five.
