fo_supper:10:4 | Mei Lin's mother is sewing at table, which she holds to be a fault in other people. A strip of white cloth, inked with the family's name and the village's, is going into the collar of her daughter's travelling jacket, so that whoever comes upon the jacket will know where it belongs. ¶ Mei Lin says it is so the forest knows where to send the bill. She says it brightly, then eats without tasting anything. Her mother bites off the thread. ¶ Ya has followed the needle all the way round. She pushes her bowl aside and holds out the neck of her own hide shirt. "Me also." Asked her family's name, she shrugs; asked her village's, she laughs. The mother thinks, grinds a little more ink, and writes. ¶ "What does mine say, scholar?" It says *guest of this house*. You tell her. She wears the collar turned outward until the bowls are empty, where it can be read.
fo_supper:10:4:away | Supper. The mother sews a name strip into a collar, and nobody asks.
fo_supper:10:5 | The instructions come with the millet, one to a serving, addressed to the guide. Mei Lin is not to sleep with her hair wet. She is not to be given river fish, which disagrees with her. She is to be made to stop when she says she is not tired. ¶ "I am twenty-six years old," Mei Lin tells the pickles, "and present." She is passed the pickles. The next instruction concerns her chest in damp weather, and at that one she leaves off being amused and looks into her bowl. ¶ Ya cannot write any of it down and has not tried. She pulls a thong off her wrist and ties a knot in it for each, hard, with her teeth. When the mother has finished there are six knots. Ya ties a seventh. ¶ "She is not to be eaten," she says. "You forgot that one." The mother folds her hands. "It was not forgotten. It is not said at table."
fo_supper:10:5:away | Supper. The mother tells you what her daughter may not eat, twice over.
fo_supper:10:6 | Between servings Mei Lin's mother asks what your people do before a journey; she wishes the thing done correctly on your side also. You tell her: they look at the sky, and they tell someone where they are going. She weighs it. "Then you have done both," she says, and fills your bowl. ¶ Mei Lin says that her own people fret, in order of seniority, and that she is well ahead of her turn. Her mother straightens a dish instead of answering. ¶ "Mine take something," says Ya, through a mouthful. "From the house. Then the house wants you back." She opens her fist. In it lies one of the glazed chopstick rests, which have been going missing. ¶ The mother counts the rests left on the table. She does not ask for that one. Mei Lin laughs, and stops, and when she believes herself unwatched slides another into her sleeve. Yuki watched, and is letting it stand.
fo_supper:10:6:away | Supper. The mother asks after your country's travelling customs, and hears you out.
# notes
- Motifs: (4) a name strip sewn into the collar, Ya asks for one and gets "guest of this house"; (5) the mother's care instructions to the guide, Ya keeps them as knots and adds a seventh; (6) customs before a journey, Ya's is taking something from the house, Mei Lin palms a chopstick rest.
- Assumed: Ya wears a hide shirt and a thong on her wrist; she has no family or village name to give; the mother writes (ink and brush in the house); Mei Lin dislikes river fish and has a weak chest in the damp (swap for any canon ailment, or cut to "her feet in damp weather"); the house owns glazed chopstick rests.
- 10:4 never says what the strip is for beyond "where it belongs"; the grim reading is left to the player. 10:5's "not to be eaten" is Ya's joke and the mother's refusal to say it.
- 10:6: James's answer (look at the sky, tell someone where you are going) is invented for his people; it contradicts nothing on the card.
- The away lines assume the household knows of a going when Ya has not been met (same caveat as run F1); 10:5:away and 10:6:away need neutral lines if not.
- Yuki appears only in 10:6, in a sentence that fits all three forms.
