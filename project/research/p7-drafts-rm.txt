"And bent." ¶ "They're made for my eyes. Yours work." ¶ "So in your country a scholar pays to see what a goatherd sees for nothing." ¶ "There's a waiting list." ¶ She has to laugh into her sleeve, glasses and all, and keeps her voice down as a penance. They sit crooked on her. You don't straighten them, and she shows no hurry to give them back.
supper_roof:2:4:r | The cold arrives before she has finished with the glasses. Halfway down the rungs she hands them back folded, in both palms, as one returns a borrowed blade, and tells you the well looked better without them. You take the bent world up the ladder to the herb loft and sleep in it.
supper_roof:2:5 | Tonight Mei Lin examines you on the contents of the herb loft, in the dark, by smell alone. ¶ She passes the sprigs across from her sleeve, one at a time. You get the mugwort. You get the dried orange peel, which she says a child would. The third is bitter and faintly of cellars, and you name the mould growing on it instead, with its habits. ¶ "That is a fault in the stock," she says. "You are not supposed to praise it." ¶ "It's doing very well." ¶ "My mother will have it out by morning." She takes the sprig back and sniffs it herself, frowning. "Which shelf?" ¶ "Third. By the window. It likes the damp." ¶ "So do you, it seems. You sleep there."
supper_roof:2:5:r | By the time the thatch is cold she has run out of sprigs and you have failed four, every one of them a plain leaf. She goes down reciting their names at you like a debt. You climb to your bed in the herb loft, wish the third shelf goodnight, and sleep beside it.
supper_roof:2:6 | Mei Lin has your white coat across her knees and is mending the cuff by feel, since a lamp would bring the lane. ¶ "Who sewed this?" ¶ "A machine." ¶ "It was not proud of the work." She bites the thread. Her stitches are smaller than the ones she picked out. "When the collectors are here it goes under the loft floor. Nobody's cousin owns a coat this white." ¶ "And after?" ¶ Her needle stops. Down in the lane a door shuts, and a dog is told to be quiet. ¶ "After, I will dig it out again, and you will tear it on a fence," she says, "and I will be very short with you." ¶ It is the nearest thing to a prayer you have heard from her.
supper_roof:2:6:r | She sews until her fingers are too cold to hold the needle, and then a little longer. The coat goes down the ladder over her arm, not yours; it is not finished, she says. The herb loft gets you in your shirtsleeves tonight, and you sleep colder, and better.
