The hunters' custom so far, by day, in the plain win text (`far_won`, shown when James is not so strong):
1. "The kill travels to Ashfall folded away and comes out whole at the hunters' feet. They bow to it without a word, as one bows to a fallen enemy. Then, with equal gravity, to you."
2. "It rides home in the hammerspace as far as the oak hollow, and on your shoulders after that, for the look of the thing. This time the hunters meet you at the oak hollow, unasked, and take the weight from you in order of age. Nobody says well done. 3. "By now the hunters have a place swept for the kill a bowshot past the oak hollow, on the hunting side, and nobody admits to the sweeping. You set it down there; they bow to it, then to you, in the order custom has settled on. One of the younger men bows a breath early and is looked at."

The wolves (degree 2), now:
1. "They are clever and quick, and they hunt as one animal that happens to own two sets of teeth. Working out that you are not prey takes them the better part of a minute. Nothing after that takes them any time at all. One of them carried a core, small and murky. ¶ Ashfall gets the rest all at once, out of the hammerspace. The hunters accept it in silence and give it the bow owed to an enemy who fell well. Afterwards, no less solemnly, they bow to you."
2. "A pair of ash-wolves, with the narrow grey manners of the kind. They open as wolves do, one wide to each hand, and you let them finish the manoeuvre, since it is the only one they own. It puts you between them, which is the flaw in it. Two needles. One wolf carried a core, murky and small. ¶ The hunters are waiting at the oak hollow, unsent for, so the wolves need go no further. You bring them out of the hammerspace there, and the hunters take them eldest man first. No one speaks. The eldest looks over the wolves, then over you, and lowers his chin a finger's breadth. You gather that this is rank."

The thornback bull (degree 2), now (James also takes two eggs from its nest):
1. "It is huge, plated and enraged, and it had been counting on those three things to carry more weight than they do. At the end it lies on its side among the ferns with its spines laid flat and its breath coming slow, and one gold eye tracks you with what, in a politer animal, would be respect. You do not leave it there to think. One needle, and the eye stops tracking. ¶ The core comes out small and murky. You bring the rest to Ashfall in the hammerspace, and two eggs out of its nest along with it. The hunters meet it without speaking and bow over it as over an enemy fallen with honour; then, just as gravely, to you."
2. "A bull the size of a woodshed, and no better informed than one. It lowers the palisade and comes on, and you allow it the first three charges, because it has plainly rehearsed them. The fourth meets a wedge of bone set where plate joins plate. It folds at the knees, lets out its steam, and is finished before it has settled into the ferns. The core comes out murky and small. ¶ It goes as far as the oak hollow folded away, and comes out there for you to shoulder, with two eggs from the nest riding on top. The hunters are there already, unsent for, and they lift the weight off you by seniority. Not a word. The eldest looks along the bull, then at you, and tips his head a finger's width. You take it for promotion, and nobody corrects you."
